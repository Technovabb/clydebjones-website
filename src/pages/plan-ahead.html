Plan ahead

Your wishes, written down. Your family, spared the guesswork.

“Is this what they would have wanted?” is a question families ask all too often. Making your wishes clear ahead of time can be a great comfort to the people you love.

What is pre-planning?

Pre-planning simply means making plans for a funeral before they are needed. You gather some family information and decide on the kind of service you want: where it should be held, and who might take part.

You work with one of our funeral directors or co-ordinators to plan, organise and record your wishes. We explain all of our products and services, so you can make informed choices.

Why plan ahead?

Most people tell us they do it to protect their family, so no one has to make hard decisions at a time of loss.

Planning ahead also gives you the time and information to make careful choices, without pressure, that are right for you and your family.

Paying ahead is your choice

Three ways to handle the cost

You can pay ahead, or simply keep your plan on file with us. It is then carried out when needed, and invoiced to the estate or family at that time.

Pay in full

Once your plan is fully paid, we guarantee the services and items you chose, whatever happens to our prices later.

Pay in instalments

Spread the cost with regular payments over a time you agree with us.

Bereavement insurance

An insurance plan made for funeral costs. Some include family members, and some cover a death away from home. We can explain how they work and point you to the right companies.

Good to know: some costs, such as newspaper notices, are not provided by us, so we cannot lock in their price. We will help you estimate them and allow for them in your plan.

Starting the conversation

How to talk to your family about it

The conversation is much easier when no one is ill. Some families talk over dinner. Others prefer a proper meeting. You know your family best.

  • Set a time. Treat it like an appointment, whether you are sharing your plans or asking a parent about theirs.
  • Ease into it. Ask, “Have you ever thought about the kind of funeral you would like?”
  • Use the moment. After a friend's funeral, talk about what you liked, or did not like.
  • Say why. Tell them you are doing this because you love them, and do not want to leave them with hard decisions.
  • Make it a family affair. Bring your children with you to meet us, if you would like.
