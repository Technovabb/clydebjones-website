Questions families ask

Frequently asked questions

Someone has just died. What do I do first?

It depends on where the death happened. If it was at home, call the police station for that district. If it was in hospital, call us and we will contact the hospital for you. See what to do first, or call us on (246) 428-9332.

What are your office hours?

Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. We are closed on Saturday and Sunday.

After-hours line: number to come

What details will you need from us?

Your loved one's full legal name (and any other names they used), date and place of birth, National Registration Number, home address, their father's name, their mother's name including her maiden name, and their occupation. See the full checklist.

How much does a funeral cost?

It depends on the choices you make, such as the casket or urn, the cars and the type of service. We explain every cost before you decide, so you stay in control of what you spend. Please call us to talk about prices.

Can family overseas watch the funeral?

Yes. The livestream plays on your loved one's tribute page, and the recording stays there afterwards. How livestreaming works.

My loved one died overseas. Can you bring them home?

Yes. Call us first. We will work with the funeral home where they died and bring them home to Barbados. From the USA or Canada, call (716) 262-2407. About repatriation.

Do we need a casket if we choose cremation?

Your loved one must be placed in a firm container that can be cremated. This can be a simple cremation case. More about cremation.

How do I write an obituary?

Your funeral co-ordinator will gladly help you write it, and arrange it with the newspaper and radio. An obituary usually includes:

  • Their full name, and the name people knew them by.
  • Where they lived.
  • Their dates of birth and passing.
  • Their close family, and whether each person is living or has died.
  • The times and places of the viewing, funeral and burial.
  • Something special about them: work, interests, achievements.
  • A charity for donations in their memory, if you would like one.
How do I send flowers?

Fill in our flower request, and a member of our staff will contact you to arrange them.

Can I plan my own funeral ahead of time?

Yes. You can record your wishes with us, and choose whether to pay ahead in full, in instalments, or not at all. About planning ahead.

National Insurance

The funeral grant

National Insurance (NIS) may pay a funeral grant towards the cost of a funeral.

  • It is paid to the person who paid for the funeral.
  • It depends on the National Insurance record of the person who died, or of their spouse.
  • You will need the original funeral receipt, in your own name.

The rules can change, so please check with National Insurance before you claim.

Wills & probate

Wills, probate and the estate

A will tells your loved ones what you want after your death. It can be updated or replaced at any time. In Barbados, you can lodge your will for safekeeping in the Barbados Depository of Wills at the Supreme Court Registry. The most recent will lodged is treated as your last will.

Probate is the legal process that confirms a will is valid, so the executor can deal with the property, pay any debts and share out what is left. If there is no will, the next of kin apply to the court for Letters of Administration.

Every estate is different. Please speak to an attorney-at-law for advice. This page is general information, not legal advice.

Grief support

You do not have to grieve alone

Grief has no timetable. Talk to family, friends, and your minister if you have one. If you need more help, we can put you in touch with a bereavement counsellor.

Signs you may need extra help, and how to help a friend

If you are thinking of harming yourself, please get help now. In an emergency in Barbados, call 511 for an ambulance or 211 for the police.

Talk to us

Call us during office hours, and we will put you in touch with someone who can help.
