Cremation

Cremation, explained simply

Choosing cremation does not limit how you say goodbye. You can still have a viewing, a service and a lasting memorial.

What happens

Cremation uses intense heat, for two to three hours, to reduce the body to bone fragments. These are then made finer and placed in an urn or a temporary container. The remains, often called ashes, usually weigh between three and six pounds.

The permit: a cremation can only take place after the Chief Medical Officer of the Ministry of Health has issued a cremation permit. We will guide you through this.

Choices before the cremation

  • Would you like a viewing or visitation first?
  • Would you like a funeral or a short service before the cremation?
  • Would your family like to be present at the cremation? Ask us what is possible.
  • Which urn would you like: metal, wood, marble, ceramic or biodegradable? And what engraving?

Do we need a casket?

Your loved one must be placed in a firm container that can be cremated. This can be a simple cremation case. If you are having a service first, ask us about a casket for the service.

Can we place keepsakes with them?

Yes. Personal items can be placed in the casket and cremated with your loved one. Some things that do not burn safely, such as batteries and electronics, must be left out. We will check with you.

After the cremation

  • Burial: the urn can be buried in a family plot, or in a new plot.
  • A niche: the urn can be placed in a niche in a columbarium, a building made for urns.
  • Sending overseas: we can help with the papers needed to send the ashes to another country.

A permanent resting place gives family a place to visit and reflect, for generations to come. Ashes kept at home can be misplaced over the years.
