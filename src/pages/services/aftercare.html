Aftercare & grief support

We are still here after the funeral

Grief has no timetable. Give yourself the time you need, and know that help is here when you want it.

There is no set time for grief

The death of someone you love can bring a flood of feelings. You may think a week, or three months, will be enough, and then life will go back to normal. But grief does not work to a timetable.

Talk to family, friends, and your minister if you have one. If you need more help, we can put you in touch with a bereavement counsellor. Just call us on (246) 428-9332.

Signs you may need extra help

Everyone who grieves feels some of these for a while. If they go on, it may be time to talk to someone who understands grief, even just to know you are on the right path.

  • You feel angry, irritable or short-tempered most of the time.
  • You feel numb, or alone, with no one to talk to.
  • You worry all the time about dying, or about losing someone else.
  • Months later, you still cannot think about anything else.
  • You keep yourself busy all the time, so you do not have to feel.
  • You are afraid to get close to people, in case you lose them too.
  • You are drinking more, taking more pills, or taking risks.
  • You are carrying too much for the rest of the family.
  • You feel stuck, and cannot move forward.

If you are thinking of harming yourself, please get help now. In an emergency in Barbados, call 511 for an ambulance or 211 for the police.

Trust your own judgement. Grief is painful enough without carrying it alone.

Helping a friend who is grieving

  • Bring a meal, on a day that suits them.
  • Help with the garden, the pets, the shopping or the laundry.
  • Offer to drive them to the cemetery.
  • Help with thank-you notes and other letters.
  • Remember the hard days: birthdays, holidays and the anniversary of the death.
  • Say their loved one's name, and share your memories.
  • Above all, listen. Your presence helps more than you know.
