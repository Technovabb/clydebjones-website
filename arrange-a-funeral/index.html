Arrange a funeral

We will guide you, one step at a time.

Losing someone you love is hard enough. Here is what to do first, and how our team will help you plan a farewell that honours them.

Call (246) 428-9332 USA & Canada (716) 262-2407

When a death occurs

What to do first depends on where it happened

You do not have to know all of this. Call us at any point, and we will talk you through it.

At home

Call the police station for the district where the death happened. In most cases, the police will bring a doctor to certify the death, and will contact the Coroner, who decides the next step.

If your loved one was not under a doctor's care, a post-mortem is usually ordered. Then call us.

In hospital

Call us. We will contact the hospital for you and arrange the documents that are needed.

In a nursing or care home

Tell the staff that you have chosen Clyde B. Jones Funeral Home. They will contact us for you.

Overseas

Call us, even when the death happens away from home. We will work with a funeral home where your loved one died, and bring them home to Barbados. How repatriation works

A simple checklist

What to do first

Take it one step at a time. We will help with each one.

  1. Call us

    Call (246) 428-9332. From the USA or Canada, call (716) 262-2407. We will make an appointment to sit down with you and plan the funeral.

  2. Tell the people who need to know

    • Family and close friends.
    • The person's employer, and your own if you need time off.
    • Insurance companies (life, health and accident).
    • Their church, lodge, union or other groups.
    • Their lawyer and accountant.

  3. Gather their details

    We will need these to prepare the paperwork:

    • Full legal name, and any other names they were known by.
    • Date and place of birth.
    • National Registration Number.
    • Home address.
    • Father's name, and mother's name, including her maiden name.
    • Occupation.

  4. Find important papers

    These may be needed for insurance, pensions and benefits:

    • The will.
    • Birth certificate, or other proof of age.
    • Marriage certificate.
    • Insurance policies.
    • Bank books, deeds and car ownership papers.
    • Income tax returns and receipts.

  5. Plan the funeral with us

    At our meeting, we will talk through:

    • Burial or cremation.
    • The service: a viewing, a funeral or memorial service, a reception.
    • The clothing your loved one will wear.
    • The minister or clergy, pallbearers, music, readings, photos and flowers.
    • A charity for donations in their memory, if you would like one.

Choices for the service

There is no one right way to say goodbye

Every funeral should be as unique as the life it honours. Your faith or culture may guide some parts of it. You can make the rest your own.

  • Viewing or visitation. Usually the night before, or just before the funeral, so friends can pay their respects.
  • Funeral service. At your church, or another place that meant something to them. The casket can be open or closed.
  • Memorial or celebration of life. Family and friends come together to remember, usually without the casket.
  • Graveside service. Final words, prayers and memories at the graveside, before or instead of an indoor service.
More about funeral planning

What you will pay for

  • The funeral itself: our services, the casket or urn, the cars, and anything else you choose.
  • Other costs, such as the clergy, florist and newspaper notices.
  • Cemetery charges, such as the plot, opening and closing the grave, and any headstone.

We explain every cost before you decide, so you stay in control of what you spend.
